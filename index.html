Wang Yan

I am currently studying Computer Science and Cognitive Science at Columbia University. Before returning to undergraduate study, I spent eight years as a technology entrepreneur and product builder across fintech infrastructure and digital publishing. Before entering technology, I trained as a civil engineer and completed a master’s thesis on Buddhist art. My background also includes film production and artistic practice, experiences that continue to shape how I think about representation and human experience.

My work looks at how AI systems build an understanding of the world and act when information is incomplete. I am especially interested in world models, computer vision and multi-agent systems, particularly how representations and constraints shape what these systems can perceive, predict, and do. I also care about building AI systems that people can better collaborate with.

Outside research, I spend time with photography, film, music, and older technologies. I am fascinated by the ways legacy cameras, games, and early interfaces shaped how people experienced technology. This site is a collection of my research projects, prototypes, code experiments, films, and visual studies.

2026 · HCI / Decision Systems / Migration Studies

Status: Completed

Case Souleymane: Interactive Asylum Case-Review System

Designed an interactive case-review system that places users in the role of an asylum adjudicator reviewing a fictional case file. The project explores how interfaces can represent incomplete evidence, uncertainty, and institutional responsibility when decisions must still be made. Rather than presenting uncertainty as a limitation to avoid, the system makes uncertainty part of the interaction itself, allowing users to experience the tension between limited information and the obligation to decide.

Role: Author

Faculty Advisor: Prof. Madeleine Dobie

Methods: YanOS Framework, narrative systems, web development

2025 · Human–AI Interaction

Paper Plane: Anonymous Letters as Shared Urban Poetry

Paper Plane is an interactive application between a poetic game and an AI-mediated writing experience. The demo video documents the application rather than serving as the work itself. In the experience, a user writes an anonymous letter, folds it into a digital paper plane, and releases it into a city. Each flight produces an AI-generated ending, turning private writing into a shared urban poem.

Role: Concept, visual Design, writing

Stack / Tools: Three.js, WebGL

Art Works

2026 · AI Cinema / Film / Visual Research

Threshold

Threshold is a short experimental narrative about flooding, memory, and the fragile line between safety and disaster. Told through a first-person body-mounted perspective in contemporary Brooklyn, the film follows a person and their dog as a dream of cherry blossoms slowly gives way to rain, rising water, and a basement flood. The piece explores thresholds as both physical and emotional boundaries: a doorway, a warning line, a flood level, and the moment when care, attachment, and danger become inseparable.

Role: Direction, editing, visual research

Stack / Tools: Final cut pro, storyboards, Kling 3.0
